# E2 holdout A–F: measured findings

Source: `AF_HOLDOUT_REPORT.md`, `summary.json` and `case_rows.jsonl` in this folder (generated by
`python -m phase3_final.holdout_af`). The stack is frozen: `recommended_stable` policy, frozen twins and
conformal scale, gate k=1 / limit 1.0. Nothing was tuned on GEANT or Kdl.

| Topology | Cases | Unsafe proposals | A unsafe accept | B–F unsafe accept | False rejects given safe proposal | Applied satisfaction A → E | Applied MLU A → E |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| GEANT | 1600 | 1386 | 0.866 | 0.000 | B 1/214, C 2/214, D 0/214, E 0/214 | 0.8802 → 0.9763 | 2.209 → 1.553 |
| Kdl | 8 | 7 | 0.875 | 0.000 | 0/1 for all | 0.8006 → 0.9294 | 5.978 → 3.911 |

1. **The gate generalizes to unseen topologies.** On both GEANT and Kdl, every twin-gated variant (B–E)
   removes all unsafe accepts. The improved-twin gates (D, E) match the physics oracle (F) on every case.
   E/F accept discordance is 0 / 1600 on GEANT and 0 / 8 on Kdl.
2. **The improved twin has a small decision-level gain over the original twin on GEANT.** The original twin
   falsely rejects 1 (B, mean-only) or 2 (C, uncertainty-aware) of the 214 safe proposals; the improved twin
   rejects none. This is the first decision-level difference between the original and improved twins; the
   development A–F run showed none. The effect is small (1–2 cases).
3. **Uncertainty adds no measurable value over mean-only on holdout.** D and E agree on every case
   (0 discordant accepts). The improved twin's mean is already oracle-accurate on these unseen graphs, so the
   uncertainty term never changes a decision. E4 tests the regime where it could.
4. **The policy does not generalize; the framework contains the damage.** On GEANT the actor's MLU is 1.49×
   shortest-path at every load level, and on Kdl 1.54×. The ratio is constant across loads, so the actor's
   split barely depends on load magnitude. Gating with fallback raises applied satisfaction from 0.880 to
   0.976 on GEANT and from 0.801 to 0.929 on Kdl, and it lowers applied MLU.
5. **Fallback cannot always restore feasibility.** 68% of GEANT cases and 5 of 8 Kdl cases still overload after
   fallback, because shortest and equal are both infeasible. At calibrated GEANT loads 0.7–0.9, the LP shows a
   feasible k=4 routing exists (LP MLU = target) while the fallback overloads. Rejection is safe relative to
   the proposal but does not find that routing (a stronger fallback is not part of this study).
6. **Overhead.** GEANT about 2 ms per gated decision. Kdl about 5 s per twin-gated decision (twin about 4 s) plus
   about 1.2 s for the policy, with the policy graph cached (older reports quote about 100 s with graph rebuilds).

Satisfaction uses P1's definition. "LP" in the report is the **min-MLU** LP over k=4 paths (SciPy HiGHS),
not P1's max-satisfaction LP. At overloaded fixed scales its satisfaction can therefore be lower than the
fallback's (GEANT fixed 1.0: LP 0.882 vs E 0.912).
